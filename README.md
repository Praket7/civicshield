# CivicShield AI

Tenant legal defense automation meets institutional accountability. Built for the people who need it most.

# Site

CivicShieldAI is CURRENTLY LIVE at cividshieldai.vercel.app

## What it does

CivicShield helps tenants facing eviction by generating legal defense documents based on their specific city and state laws. Upload a notice, get back a real answer you can bring to court — with the actual statute codes that apply to your situation, not generic advice from the internet.

On the other side of the coin, the platform maps out the institutional relationships that enable predatory housing practices. City council members, real estate developers, zoning boards, lobbyists, shell companies — all the connections that are technically public record but that nobody has time to piece together. When we detect patterns (a council member voting for seven variances that all benefit the same LLC, for example), the system flags them as anomalies and makes them visible to everyone.

Right now the platform covers 22 US cities with real data — real council members, real developers, real state-specific tenant protection laws, and real neighborhood eviction rates.

## What's inside

**For tenants:**
- Upload any eviction notice (PDF or photo) and get a legal defense document generated automatically
- AI chat assistant that answers tenant rights questions with specific statute citations for your city
- Risk calculator that scores your situation and tells you what protections apply
- Five legal document templates — eviction answer, habitability complaint, deposit demand, rent overcharge petition, harassment complaint
- Court preparation guides and a directory of free legal aid organizations with real phone numbers

**For communities:**
- Interactive graph showing who's connected to who in your city's housing power structure
- Geographic heatmap of eviction hotspots by neighborhood
- Anonymous incident reporting system where tenants can flag illegal lockouts, harassment, and code violations
- Anomaly detection that highlights suspicious patterns in zoning decisions and political spending

**For accountability:**
- Every city's institutional graph shows the actual relationships between council members, developers, PACs, zoning actions, and tenant organizations
- Centrality analysis flags entities with unusually high influence across categories
- Shell company tracking connects anonymous LLCs to their parent companies and eviction filing patterns

## How the legal documents work

This was a deliberate design choice — the AI is only used for two things: answering questions in the chat and extracting names/dates/addresses from uploaded documents. The actual legal defense documents are generated by a deterministic template system (we call it the FSM compiler) that slots verified information into rigid, pre-written legal templates. The AI never writes a single word of the court document. That means there are no hallucinated statute numbers, no fabricated legal citations, and no made-up procedural rules in anything a tenant might actually hand to a judge.

## Tech stack

- **Frontend:** Next.js 16 with App Router, Tailwind CSS, TypeScript
- **Database:** PostgreSQL on Neon, Drizzle ORM
- **AI:** Groq API running Llama 3.3-70b for chat, Llama 3.1-8b for entity extraction
- **Visualization:** react-force-graph-2d for institutional networks, React-Leaflet for geographic heatmaps
- **Auth:** JWT with bcrypt password hashing
- **Deployment:** Vercel (serverless), Neon (serverless Postgres)

Total infrastructure cost: $0/month on free tiers.

## The important disclaimer

CivicShield provides legal information, not legal advice. No attorney-client relationship is created. The documents generated are informational templates — anyone facing eviction should consult with a licensed attorney. Many cities offer free legal representation through Right to Counsel programs, and the platform includes a directory of those resources.
